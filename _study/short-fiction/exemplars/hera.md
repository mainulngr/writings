# হেরার বিস্ময়যাত্রা — study file (adult band)

Faisal Rocky (ফয়সাল রকি), Facebook post, Oct 2026.

**Rights:** copyrighted. The full text is a **private study copy** at
`data/exemplars/modern-bangla/hera.txt` (local-only data repo, no remote).
Only short quotes appear here. Never republish, never ship.

**Length:** ~1,450 words — inside the adult band (800–3,000).

## What it is

A university girl rides a rickshaw to campus under a black chador, hiding a
bodily change she cannot name to anyone. A handsome stranger in a bright blue
shirt looks at her; she is unsettled, flattered, ashamed. A phone call with her
ex-roommate delivers the ordinary texture of her life — the commute, the rent,
a boy named Anis. Then a motorcycle rams the rickshaw, a public argument
escalates, the "gentleman" rescuer turns into a foul-mouthed brawler, the
motorcycle falls on the rickshaw, and her chador snags on a hood-nut and comes
off. The crowd sees. She spreads her wings and flies.

## Structure — how it actually moves

| Beat | What it does |
|---|---|
| 1. Light through the hood | Establishes discomfort as physical, not stated. The chador is named in line one; the secret is not. |
| 2. The stare | Introduces wanting-to-be-seen and fear-of-being-seen in the same glance. |
| 3. The grandfather's fairy tale | Plants the myth: grandmother was a fairy, her wings were cut, she was kept. Consolation story, told as charm. |
| 4. The cream memory | The skin-colour thread; self-mockery instead of self-pity. |
| 5. The phone call | Ordinary life, exposition folded into gossip; Anis introduced as warmth she refuses to show. |
| 6. The collision | The world enters: class, machismo, performative chivalry, a crowd. |
| 7. The rescuer collapses | The blue-shirt man's politeness lasts two lines, then he is swearing at a stranger's parents. |
| 8. The chador snags | The reveal is mechanical — a nut on the hood, not a decision. |
| 9. The flight | No resolution, no explanation. She leaves upward, badly. |

## Devices worth importing

**20. The draped secret** (cataloged from this story) — the condition is in
every paragraph and never named; a garment carries it. When the garment fails,
the reveal is caused by an object, not by a choice. Lets you write the body,
illness, shame or taboo without stating it — which is the restraint register
already in `voice.md`.

**21. The legend that comes true** (cataloged from this story) — a family
folktale told early as consolation returns at the climax as literal physics.
The magic is *earned* because the reader was handed the myth before the
miracle. Distinct from device 18 (an image returning): here a **belief system**
returns.

Secondary moves, not yet numbered:

- **The rescuer who collapses** — the handsome intervenor's decency has a
  two-line shelf life. This is the Tom/Jerry thesis dramatized: the hero frame
  dissolves the moment it costs something.
- **The crowd as X-ray** — a two-page street argument exposes rickshaw-puller,
  motorcyclist, "gentleman", and bystanders in one frame, each by their own
  speech. No narration of class; only voices.
- **Dialect inside a literary frame** — "দেইখ্যা", "কইতে হইবো", "বাইয়া থাকেন"
  sit beside "তীক্ষ্ণভাবে বিঁধলো". The contrast does the work; neither register
  is apologized for.
- **Ending on motion, not meaning** — the last line is "খানিকটা টালমাটাল!" —
  a hook, not a verdict.

## The real subject

Not wings. **Being looked at.** The story tracks three kinds of gaze:
the stranger's (desire), the crowd's (exposure), the grandfather's
(mythologizing — "তুই আরেকটা পরী", which hurts more than the slur).
The dark-skin thread — the fairness creams, "শ্যামলা" said to her face,
"গায়ের রঙ কালা হইলেও ফেস কাটিং ভালো" said in the street — is the spine.
The wings answer *being seen* by leaving. They do not answer being dark.
Whether that is the point (escape is not acceptance) or a gap is the best
question the story leaves open.

## Honest weaknesses

- **Backstory by phone call.** Joyita's call carries the rent, the roommate
  history and Anis in one convenient packet. Functional, visible as machinery.
- **The escalation is schematic.** Slur → insult the parents → brawl is three
  clean steps; real street fights are messier and funnier.
- **The accident runs long.** ~400 words of argument before the reveal dilutes
  the story's actual subject.
- **"পৃথিবী স্তব্ধ হয়ে গেল"** is a stock phrase in an otherwise precise piece.
- **It reads as chapter one.** `#হেরার_বিস্ময়যাত্রা` suggests a serial; the
  ending is a hook. Strong as a serial opening, incomplete as a standalone.

## Study questions (for later)

1. Where exactly does the story tell you what the change is — and does it ever?
2. The fairy tale is 150 words early and pays off the entire climax. Could you
   plant something that cheaply in your own piece?
3. Track every mention of colour. Which ones are said by others, which by her?
4. What would the story lose if the wings were a metaphor instead of literal?
5. The rescuer's collapse: how many lines does his decency survive? Why is that
   funnier and crueller than making him a villain from the start?

## When to study it

**After Module 3** (turns and endings) — its engine is withholding plus a
folklore payoff. Revisit after **Module 4** for the crowd scene and the
dialect-in-literary-frame dialogue.

## Re-siting seeds for adult fiction here

- A character hides a change under clothing or ritual; an object betrays it.
- A family myth (a cursed tree, a river that takes one child a generation)
  turns literal at the worst moment.
- A public confrontation where every participant is exposed by their own
  speech, and the "good" one breaks first.
