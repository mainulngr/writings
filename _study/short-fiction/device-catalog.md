# Device catalog — storytelling imports for Bangla fiction

The core artifact of this lane. Not translations — **the apparatus** of a
story (premise, structure, turn, constraint) lifted from world literature
and re-sited inside Bangla culture and `voice.md`'s register. One device
at a time, slowly; each entry is also a story seed for the pipeline.

World literature **includes Bangla** — three source rings, three lessons:

- **Bangla classics** — deep structure in the home register.
- **World literature** (via English/other translations) — devices Bangla
  fiction lacks.
- **Modern highly-rated Bangla** — the contemporary register: how stories
  that win *today's* audience sound. Ratings are a taste signal, not a
  craft certificate — dissect for *why they hit*, not just how they're made.

Format per entry: **device** · exemplar · why it fits · re-siting seed ·
source access (PD = public domain, free to corpus; modern = buy/library).

## Bangla classics

### 1. Restrained tenderness across the gap
- **Exemplar:** Tagore, "Kabuliwallah" (কাবুলিওয়ালা) — PD (1892).
- **Device:** cross-cultural tenderness told with restraint; compression
  of years into one returning figure.
- **Why it fits:** his register is already Tagore-adjacent — the lesson is
  *how* Tagore earns sentiment without syrup.
- **Re-siting seed:** a returning vendor/figure whose bond with a child
  outlasts circumstances — retold in today's Dhaka.

### 2. The educated wit
- **Exemplar:** Parashuram/Rajshekhar Basu — modern copyright edge
  (d. 1960).
- **Device:** dry learned humor + compression; satire that stays amiable.
- **Why it fits:** direct ancestor of his gentle-satire lane.
- **Re-siting seed:** a Parashuram-inflected narrator observing a modern
  absurdity — tech hype or social-media performance.

### 3. Empathy past the human
- **Exemplar:** Sarat Chandra, "Mahesh" (মহেশ) — PD (d. 1938).
- **Device:** full interiority granted to an animal; poverty rendered
  without melodrama.
- **Why it fits:** his empathy-extends-to-animals worldview, in canon form.
- **Re-siting seed:** a city stray or rickshaw-puller's companion —
  interiority granted, never sentimentalized.

### 4. Village texture as authority
- **Exemplar:** Hasan Azizul Haque (হাসান আজিজুল হক) — modern.
- **Device:** deep regional specificity that reads universal; soil-level
  detail carrying big themes.
- **Why it fits:** the specificity>universality proof — the opposite of
  generic writing.
- **Re-siting seed:** one concrete neighborhood/childhood detail rendered
  so exactly it stops being local.

### 5. The effortless register
- **Exemplar:** Humayun Ahmed — modern.
- **Device:** mass-appeal voice; literary-feeling writing that reads
  effortless. Imperfect craft, perfect reach.
- **Why it fits:** THE audience-register master — the lesson is
  accessibility mechanics, not prose quality.
- **Re-siting seed:** diagnose *why* his openings hook ordinary readers;
  apply the mechanism, not the manner.

### 6. Density and ellipsis (advanced)
- **Exemplar:** Akhtaruzzaman Elias (আখতারুজ্জামান ইলিয়াস) — modern.
- **Device:** the unsaid inside Bangla syntax; elliptical compression —
  meaning lands in gaps.
- **Why it fits:** hard-mode of the implication craft; aspirational, not
  early modules.
- **Re-siting seed:** reserved for later — visit after Module 4.

## Modern vernacular (rated-cream method)

No named entries yet — this ring is a *method*, not a canon: when a
modern Bangla story is highly rated (Pratilipi top lists, little-mag
authors publishing on FB, Prothom Alo-type venues), dissect it twice —
once for craft, once for *why the audience bit*. Entries get added as
real exemplars surface; never seeded from hearsay.

## World literature (via translation)

### 7. The cosmic premise made playful
- **Exemplar:** Italo Calvino, *Cosmicomics* (Italian) — modern.
- **Device:** take a scientific fact, narrate it as a lived memory
  ("When the Moon was closer, we played on it").
- **Why it fits:** his cosmic-awe theme — Calvino proves vastness can be
  *playful*, not only reverent.
- **Re-siting seed:** a village childhood where the monsoon river is a
  cosmic neighbor — "যখন চাঁদটা কাছে ছিল" as opening.

### 8. The palm-of-the-hand moment
- **Exemplar:** Yasunari Kawabata, *Palm-of-the-Hand Stories* (Japanese).
- **Device:** a complete emotional world compressed into one small
  object/moment, 1–3 pages.
- **Why it fits:** `brishtir_opokkha` already does this (the radio). This
  is validation + sharpening, not new territory.
- **Re-siting seed:** a courtyard object carrying a whole relationship —
  the worn দড়ি on a village swing.

### 9. The non-ending
- **Exemplar:** Chekhov, Garnett translations (Russian) — PD.
- **Device:** story ends mid-life, deliberately unresolved; the reader
  completes it.
- **Why it fits:** his হয়তো/দেখা যায় hedged realism is already Chekhovian.
- **Re-siting seed:** a bazaar scene that simply continues after the last
  line — the ending arrives off-page.

### 10. The engineered turn
- **Exemplar:** O. Henry (American) — PD.
- **Device:** design the final-line reframe first; build backward so it
  lands inevitable, not gimmicky.
- **Why it fits:** social formats reward the turn — his gentle landing
  stays primary, but the *engineering discipline* transfers to drabbles.
- **Re-siting seed:** a last line that quietly re-frames a whole
  micro-story (reframe, never gotcha).

### 11. The absurd-with-heart flash
- **Exemplar:** Etgar Keret (Hebrew) — modern.
- **Device:** surreal premise treated deadpan-mundane; emotional payoff
  smuggled inside the absurdity.
- **Why it fits:** micro-fiction's current master; matches his gentle
  humor without cruelty.
- **Re-siting seed:** a জিন who only does small favors for middle-class
  families — narrated flat, like utility billing.

### 12. The metaphysical game
- **Exemplar:** Borges (Argentine) — modern.
- **Device:** one impossible premise run to its logical conclusion in
  report/essay-story form (the Library, the Lottery).
- **Why it fits:** his reflective-intellectual register — stories that
  *think*.
- **Re-siting seed:** a government office where files decide fates —
  told as an official circular.

### 13. The fable engine
- **Exemplars:** Aesop, **Panchatantra**, Zen koans, Mulla Nasruddin — all PD.
- **Device:** ultra-compressed narrative machines built for retelling.
- **Why it fits:** Panchatantra is a *South Asian* invention that
  conquered world literature — reverse-importing it home is culturally
  resonant, not foreign.
- **Re-siting seed:** a Nasruddin-type figure re-sited as a Bengali
  village wit — a recurring fool-wise character for the gentle-satire
  lane.

### 14. The precise miniature
- **Exemplar:** Lydia Davis (American) — modern.
- **Device:** one line to one page; exact observation IS the story;
  grammar as form.
- **Why it fits:** his styled-bg variants are already this form —
  studying Davis formalizes a habit into a craft.
- **Re-siting seed:** Bangla one-to-three-line pieces where precision is
  the entire game.

### 15. The humane speculation
- **Exemplars:** Ted Chiang, Ken Liu (Chinese-American) — modern.
- **Device:** one rigorous "what if" + deep emotional core; speculation
  serving feeling, never tech for its own sake.
- **Why it fits:** warmth + curiosity in one shape — the model for the
  premise engine in course Module 5.
- **Re-siting seed:** a flood that arrives on a fixed schedule a village
  has quietly normalized — climate-lit without preaching.

### 16. The constraint as engine
- **Exemplars:** two-sentence horror, #vss365, drabble tradition — PD/open.
- **Device:** a hard formal constraint forces invention; the limit IS the
  generator.
- **Why it fits:** Bangla folk tradition has native analogs — ছড়া,
  দোহা, compressed proverb forms. Constraint craft is already home-grown.
- **Re-siting seed:** Bangla two-line stories; a ছড়া-shaped micro-fiction
  series.

## Using the catalog

- Pick one device → read the exemplar → annotate the mechanism → write a
  Bangla re-sited version → `voice-check` → artifact in `stories/`.
- Rate each attempt: what traveled, what broke — the catalog learns which
  imports survive the cultural crossing.
- Slow lane: one device at a time; the list grows only when something is
  genuinely worth importing.
